Explore Riverside

ACM SIGSPATIAL 2026 is held in Riverside, CA. Riverside and the wider Inland Southern California region are homelands of Cahuilla, Tongva, Luiseño, and Serrano peoples. The Eureka and Gold Rush story is one chapter in California’s history, not its beginning. Read UC Riverside’s land acknowledgment.

See the landmark Mission Inn and surrounding historic district. The Mission Inn Foundation offers guided downtown walking tours, with dates and availability that vary.

  • Tour schedule: Check upcoming dates and reserve in advance.

Hiking · short drive

Mount Rubidoux Park

From the Convention Center: About 1.6 mi to recommended parking · 7–10 min drive.

Choose from paved paths and dirt trails with broad views of Riverside. Plan a ride to the trailhead and bring water; summer heat can make midday hiking uncomfortable.

  • Hours: Dawn to dusk.

  • Trail: The park itself is closed to vehicles; continue on foot from the lot.

Mission

SIGSPATIAL DEI/BPC’s mission is to promote, guide, and sustain diversity, equity, and inclusion across the ACM SIGSPATIAL community while broadening participation in computing through our activities and publications. We strive to adopt a welcoming and inclusive mindset towards individuals of all backgrounds. We aim to discourage discriminatory behavior and support underrepresented groups through resources such as mentorship, networking opportunities, and attendance at our annual conferences. Our initiative is in collaboration with D&I in DB, following the ACM policy against harassment and processes for reporting unacceptable behavior.

Code of Conduct

Statement of Intent


Our goal is to promote a positive, diverse, and inclusive environment within the ACM SIGSPATIAL community, creating the grounds to foster professional growth, facilitate networking, and maintain productive professional relationships among our members, regardless of age, gender identity, race, ethnicity, socioeconomic background, country of origin, religion, sexual orientation, physical ability, education, and work experience. We expect all our members to strive to abide by and help promote these guidelines.

Expected Behavior


  • Treat others with respect. We expect all members to treat others respectfully. Even when we hold strong opinions, we should treat others’ views with dignity and courtesy. Understand that disagreements and misunderstandings may occur, but they need to be discussed and resolved in a manner that maintains respectful conduct.
  • Show empathy. Empathize different members’ backgrounds and experiences. Discussion or mention of sensitive issues may trigger negative feelings and distress to others; understand that different life experiences may impact others’ perception of certain topics. When discussing sensitive topics, respect their gravity, and do not make light of the issues with jokes or diminishing comments.
  • Embrace differences. Respect other cultures, including official religious observances and personal habits to clothing. Do not try to impose your own standards upon others. Be aware that habits and behaviors you consider benign may not be viewed as such by others. Displays of affection, such as hugging or touching, may not be acceptable in some cultures. Avoid invading others’ personal space.
  • Embrace diversity. Treat different backgrounds as a source of strength and enrichment. Explicitly seek diversity, especially when you have the power to shape representation in highly visible positions and groups, such as program committees, keynote speakers, panel participants, etc. While English is often the dominant language within our interactions, our community is largely international. Respect that English is not the native language of many of our members.

Examples of Unacceptable Behaviors


  • Misgendering or using incorrect names, especially on purpose.
  • Unwelcome comments, especially after requests to stop.
  • Discrimination, harassment, or threats of violence.
  • Offensive and disrespectful comments regarding others’ culture, gender, sexual orientation, ethnicity, age, physical appearance, and religion.
  • Microaggressions that intentionally or unintentionally patronize or denigrate members of traditionally marginalized groups.
  • Use of racially-charged or coded language.
  • Mocking someone’s appearance, culture, clothing, accent, or country of origin.

Reporting a Problem


Please refer to the SIGSPATIAL DEI/BPC Standing Committee when an unacceptable behavior happens. Also, a target can report to event chairs, the executives of respective conferences, or other event organizers who have been designated to handle such complaints. The conference executives make the final decision on the consequences of unaccepted behaviors and take action accordingly. They help a target understand the process and possible consequences in a confidential manner. If, with full information, a target decides to report, then the SIGSPATIAL DEI/BPC Standing Committee will assist in filing an official complaint and follow up with the conference and ACM SIGSPATIAL executives on appropriate actions to execute. This also applies when a witness becomes aware of an unacceptable behavior directed at another member of the community. Our code of conduct is adapted from the one in D&I in DB, which mirrors closely the Policy against Harassment at ACM Activities. Should you have any questions, please reach out to the SIGSPATIAL DEI/BPC Standing Committee.

Past Events

BPC Event @ SIGSPATIAL 2025

The BPC conference committee launched the BPC Mentoring Program at ACM SIGSPATIAL '25 to strengthen connections across the community, involving 4 organizers, 20 mentors, and 36 mentees. The program paired mentors with small groups of 2–3 mentees and connected them through a Day 1 mentoring lunch, coffee break interactions, and optional informal gatherings, with a focus on supporting participants from underrepresented groups and first-time participants.

DEI/BPC Event @ SIGSPATIAL 2024

The DEI/BPC conference committee co-organized the DEI/BPC event at ACM SIGSPATIAL '24 with SpatialWomen, involving 44 participants, including students, 3 organizers, 10 mentors assigned in advance, and researchers from academia, industry, and national labs. We hosted a lunchtime event on Day 1, where mentors shared their experiences with junior researchers and answered their questions.

Eureka! A community effort that keeps growing.

A growing commitment in ACM SIGSPATIAL over years, more than recent DEI/BPC events and programs: support for student participation and networking, with researcher gatherings and mentoring. These efforts build on one another through connection.
